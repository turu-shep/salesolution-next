## Who we're writing for (and why my earlier copy missed)

The buyer is a **problem-aware, NOT solution-aware** local-service owner (roofer/dentist). They feel the pain (missed calls, leads that ghost, "spent on marketing, can't tell what worked") but have no name for the fix and have never heard "engine vs fuel" or "system-attributed revenue." They are **time-poor, agency-burned, hype-allergic, price-sensitive, afraid of lock-in**, and **not marketers** — any unexplained acronym (GEO, map pack, schema, PMS, BAA) is friction.

Two consequences (Eugene Schwartz): they're **high market-sophistication** — they've stopped believing louder promises, so the page must turn on a **credible mechanism**, not a bigger claim. And there's **no real proof yet**, so the spine can't lean on testimonials.

## The spine: "Leak-to-Engine Story" (SB7 backbone)

**StoryBrand SB7** (owner = hero, us = guide), opened with a **PAS hook**, pivoting on a **unique-mechanism** beat (the leak → engine-vs-fuel), proven by **logic + the owner's own numbers** (not testimonials), closed with a **free-audit-as-diagnosis** arc.

The single biggest change vs today: **promote "The Leak" to screen 2.** Today the page explains the solution (How it works, Get found) *before* the problem lands — an AIDA stage-skip. Make them **feel the bleed** before showing the machine.

**New order:** Hook → **LEAK** → MECHANISM → PLAN → PROOF-BY-LOGIC → OFFER+GUARANTEE → slim FAQ → AUDIT. Same components, re-sequenced. `Get found` folds into the Plan; `VerticalFork` demotes into the hero as a self-qualifier; the heavy objections move out of the FAQ into the beats where the reader forms them.

## Tone rules (the voice)

- **First-person "I", all the way.** "I answer every call." Never swing to "Our team leverages…" (current bug).
- **Numbers before adjectives.** "47 hours is the average reply — by then the job's booked with whoever picked up." Not "lightning-fast response."
- **Zero marketer jargon in the story spine.** "Shows up when people search for a roofer near them," not "GEO / map pack / schema / system-driven." Keep those terms only on the dashboard/PROVE label.
- **Name what you DON'T do, early and specifically** — fastest trust signal for a burned owner: "No markup on your ads. No reselling your leads. No 12-month lock-in."
- **Handle each objection the instant it forms**, in the next sentence — the "even if" stomper: "Your phone gets answered 24/7 — even if you're on a roof."
- **Feel-felt-found, de-scripted** to operator voice, not 1970s sales.
- **Anchor price against the size of the leak before the number appears.**
- **Calm. Max one exclamation on the whole page (prefer zero), one em-dash per paragraph. No manufactured urgency / countdowns / false scarcity** — reads as scammy to this buyer.
- **Proof beside the claim it backs**, never quarantined.
- **Reframe every replacement fear as additive** — ads guy, Google rep, front-desk staff all stay and perform better.

## VSL: text-first, optional hero video

Keep the page **text-first**; add the VSL as an **optional transitional CTA in the hero** ("Watch the 90-second version," owner-face thumbnail beside "Book a Revenue Leak Audit"). No autoplay, no gating. SB7's Direct + Transitional CTA pair: the ready buyer books; the not-ready, burned buyer watches a real person first (fastest trust while there are no testimonials). Page must work fully without the video.

**Recordable ~60–90s script (owner-to-owner, low-hype):** Hook (pain in his words) → Stakes (3 leaks) → Mechanism (ads are fuel, I build the engine) → Plan in one breath → Risk reversal (guarantee verbatim) → single CTA (the audit). Numbers before adjectives, ≤1 exclamation, marked pauses "like sheet music."

Secondary (later): a "what happens on the audit call" clip at the close; a 20–30s dashboard screen-share at screen 5.

## Biggest risks (honest)

1. **No real proof anywhere** — a hard-coded chart labeled "proof" can read as the opposite to this buyer. Mitigate: lean screen 5 on the calculator (his numbers) + cited third-party stats + the falsifiable guarantee until first-cohort data fills the PROOF-SLOTs.
2. **"System-attributed revenue" is a constructed term** the buyer must trust us to measure. Show the measurement as plain math right before the guarantee, or the guarantee reads as riggable.
3. **Re-sequencing is non-trivial in code** — TheLeak's closer assumes the owner runs ads ("money you already spent to make the phone ring"); promoting it to screen 2 (before the ads-are-optional mechanism) requires editing that copy so it doesn't contradict screen 3.
4. **VSL credibility rests entirely on the owner's delivery** (no testimonials) — script it tightly; page must stand without it.
5. **In-story objection handling must be one tight "even if" line each**, or screens 3/4/6 bloat and the narrative momentum is lost again.
6. **One pillar, two verticals** — over-specifying roofing alienates dentists. The hero self-qualifier + per-vertical pages carry the specificity; the pillar stays dual-fit.
