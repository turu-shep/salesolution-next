## 1. "Citation engineering" collision check — ~~FREE TO OWN~~ → IN ACTIVE USE (reframe, don't coin)

The flagged gap from the assessment is resolved (verified live, June 2026):

- The exact phrase has **no owner in the GEO/AI-search context**. The generic SERP is
  university library guides about citing sources in IEEE/ASME style — zero commercial
  competition.
- Nearest claimant: GenOptima pushes the 3-word variant "Citation Outcome Engineering"
  via self-ranking listicles — low authority, no trade-press pickup, and they did not
  claim the 2-word phrase.
- Academic legitimization without claim: arXiv 2603.29979 ("Structural Feature
  Engineering for Generative Engine Optimization: How Content Structure Shapes Citation
  Behavior").
- "Citation engineer" as a job title: **zero usage found** — the role-title lane is also
  empty (consistent with the roles research).

**Disambiguation duty:** SEO-literate readers will initially misread it as local-SEO
"citation building" (Whitespark/BrightLocal own that meaning). The first sentence of our
definition must explicitly say "not local citation building." Avoid framing that sounds
like gaming (academic "citation manipulation" has negative connotations) — frame as
*earning and structuring for* citations.

**Action:** coin both "citation engineering" (practice) and "citation engineer" (role) as
umbrella terms; define first-sentence-neutral; cross-link GEO/AEO as parents; seed usage
on every service page and the careers wiki so the term's entity home is salesolution.net.
**A coined term nobody repeats is pure vanity — budget for seeding (LinkedIn, guest
posts, outreach) or deprioritize.**

## 2. Why a glossary wins (evidence)

- Wikipedia takes ~26% of LLM citations (Weglot 6,844-citation study), but the current
  winners for GEO-niche definitional queries ("AI share of voice," "AI visibility") are
  **tiny-DR tool glossaries** (Hall, LLM Pulse, Qwairy, Reaudit, RankZero) — proof that
  low-DR sites win definitional AI citations when nobody big competes.
- Pages answering fan-out sub-queries are reported **161% more likely to be cited** —
  each entry should carry "X vs Y" and "how to measure X" subheads.

## 3. Entry template (the structure playbook)

One URL per term — never a mega-page (chunk-level retrieval needs self-contained pages).
Thematic hubs, not flat A–Z. Per entry:

1. **H1 = the term.** First sentence: one neutral, promo-free answer to "What is X?",
   short enough to be quoted verbatim into an AI answer.
2. Why it matters — **always with an industrial e-commerce example** (vertical
   saturation lives here; pull from [04-niches.md](04-niches.md)).
3. Concrete example → How to measure/implement → Related terms → 2–3 genuine FAQs.
4. Tables for comparisons (highly extractable); one idea per paragraph.
5. Schema: `DefinedTerm` per page, `DefinedTermSet` on hub, breadcrumbs; `FAQPage` only
   where FAQs are real; `sameAs` to Wikipedia/Wikidata for established entities.
6. Stable terminology: one canonical phrase per concept everywhere; aliases get short
   entries canonicalizing to the main one.

(Implementation details in [06-wiki-architecture.md](06-wiki-architecture.md).)

explanations.
