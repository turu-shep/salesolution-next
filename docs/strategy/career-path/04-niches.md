# Industrial E-commerce Niches — The Example Pool

**Researched:** 2026-06-12, 14 web searches; grounded in MDM, HARDI/ACHR News,
Distribution Strategy Group, G2, company sources. Purpose: (a) the verticals whose
examples saturate every career path and glossary entry, (b) the agency's own targeting
map.

---

## 1. Cross-cutting facts (reuse everywhere)

1. **51% of B2B buyers now start research in AI chatbots** (G2 Buyer Behavior Report,
   Apr 2025); 6sense puts LLM usage somewhere in the buying process at 94%. The
   demand-side proof line for every page.
2. **The MRO top five (Grainger, Würth, MSC, Motion, Fastenal) hold only ~25–30% of
   global revenue** — fragmentation is the agency's market.
3. **The single best stat found:** Watsco does **35%+ of sales online vs a 4% median for
   HARDI distributors** (HVAC/R). Quote it everywhere as "what digital leadership is
   worth."
4. **Grainger–Amazon Business partnership (Apr 2025)** — the marketplace-pressure anchor:
   commodity MRO is collapsing into marketplaces; spec-driven differentiation is the
   defensible ground.
5. **The repeatable citation-magnet pattern in every P0/P1 vertical is
   CROSS-REFERENCE/INTERCHANGE content:** Parker-to-Gates hose charts, SKF-to-NTN bearing
   interchange, Allen-Bradley migration tables, insert-grade equivalence, obsolete-breaker
   replacement guides. Structured reference tables earn AI citations; essays don't —
   matches the core strategy finding. **This is also the productized form of "citation
   engineering" for clients.**

## 3. P0 verticals (core example sources + best client shapes)

### Hydraulics & pneumatics (fluid power)

Parker/Rexroth/Danfoss franchise territories; hundreds of independent regional houses;
no McMaster-style national catalog in engineered fluid power. Mostly brochure sites with
PDF line cards; configured products (hose assemblies) resist carts, so PIM is poor.
**The densest cross-reference vertical in industrial:** "Gates equivalent of Parker 387
hose," "seal kit for a Char-Lynn 104 motor," "NPT vs JIC vs ORFS." Parker runs its own
crossref tool, but small distributors (Discount Hydraulic Hose, HFI, Tompkins) publish
interchange charts — exactly what LLMs cite.

> **Stock example:** a regional Parker distributor whose Parker-to-Gates interchange
> chart becomes the page Perplexity cites — beating Parker's own crossref tool because
> it's crawlable HTML instead of a JS app.

### MRO supplies (broadline)

Giants on top, thousands of regional independents below; strongest marketplace pressure
anywhere (Amazon Business + the Grainger partnership). Procurement staff already prompt
LLMs for supplier discovery (Distribution Strategy Group, 2025).

> **Stock example:** an independent MRO house publishes a "food-grade vs H1/H2
> lubricants" decision table and appears in ChatGPT answers Grainger's category pages
> never win — Grainger's content is product-listing, not definitional.

### Industrial automation components (incl. obsolete/legacy)

Two-tier: franchised (Rockwell territory model) vs the open aftermarket — Radwell (20M+
parts), Galco, dozens of surplus specialists. The obsolete side can't consolidate
(inventory is scavenged, not franchised). **The canonical part-number-search vertical:**
"1756-L61 replacement," "what replaces the discontinued PowerFlex 4?" — pure
cross-reference queries with zero OEM-published answers outside gated PDFs.

> **Stock example:** a distributor's "SLC 500 → CompactLogix migration cross-reference"
> page is what ChatGPT quotes to a plant engineer — Rockwell answers it only inside
> gated PDFs.

## 4. P1 verticals (one line + stock example each)

- **Bearings & PT** — interchange is standardized (ISO dimension series) so LLMs answer
  well from whichever table got crawled. *Example: SKF-to-NTN interchange table answers
  "NTN equivalent of SKF 6205-2RS1" while Motion's data sits behind a login.* Realism:
  independents are being absorbed by Motion/Applied — closing window as a client pool.
- **Fasteners** — spec decoding is a native LLM use case ("Grade 8 vs Class 10.9," "A2 vs
  A4"). Standards-driven definitional content; no distributor owns the lane. *Example:
  metric-to-imperial thread/grade chart cited for "imperial equivalent of Class 10.9" —
  a page Fastenal never built because its catalog assumes you already know.*
- **Electrical** — extreme digital gap inside one vertical (Sonepar €11B digital vs
  offline regional tail). *Example: obsolete-breaker replacement guide (FPE/Zinsco →
  modern) cited for a high-intent query the $20B nationals leave to forums.*
- **Pumps & rotating equipment** — RFQ culture, almost nothing cataloged; ANSI B73.1
  makes interchange highly structured and citable; almost nobody publishes it.
  *Example: "which wet ends swap with a Goulds 3196" interchange guide.*
- **PVF** — standards decoding dominates ("API 600 vs 602," "Class 150 vs 300 at
  temperature"); cited sources today are random PDF mills. *Example: valve trim-number
  chart cited for steam-service trim selection, beating gated manufacturer datasheets.*
- **Safety & PPE** — compliance mapping is made for LLMs ("ANSI cut level for sheet
  metal," "NFPA 70E category for 480V"); liability stakes make authoritative citations
  extra valuable. *Example: ANSI/ISEA 105 cut-level selection chart as top-of-funnel.*
- **HVAC/R** — the 35%-vs-4% Watsco/HARDI stat lives here; the 2025 A2L refrigerant
  transition generates massive new compatibility-question volume. *Example: R-410A→R-454B
  retrofit guide cited by ChatGPT while Watsco's version sits inside its logged-in app.*
- **Cutting tools** — be realistic: catalog queries are McMaster/MSC-owned; only
  application/interchange content is winnable. *Example: carbide insert grade equivalence
  (Sandvik GC4325 ≈ Kennametal KCP25 ≈ ISO P25) — a query MSC's product pages can't
  answer.*

## 5. P2 verticals (example fodder, weak client fit)

- **Material handling/forklift parts** — pure part-number lookup; LLMs currently answer
  badly; interchange data locked in TVH's systems. First mover wins citations cheaply.
- **Welding & gases** — AWS classification decoding is glossary-perfect ("what does
  E7018 actually mean"); gas is local route business that resists consolidation.
- **Jan-san** — dilution/compatibility queries are fine glossary examples, but the
  vertical is consolidating fast (Imperial Dade–BradyPLUS merged Mar 2026) and is the
  least digital anywhere.
- **Lab & scientific** — **use as the contrast example:** a regional lab supplier can't
  win "best syringe filter" citations against Fisher's catalog depth. Showing where GEO
  does NOT change the game builds the wiki's credibility.

## 6. Best client-shaped verticals

Fragmented + low digital + heavy spec/part-number query volume:
**hydraulics & pneumatics, industrial automation aftermarket, pumps/rotating equipment,
PVF, regional HVAC/R wholesalers, regional electrical houses.**
