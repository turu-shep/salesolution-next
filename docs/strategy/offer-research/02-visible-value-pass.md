## 0. The one-paragraph read

All three verticals deserve the framing — the buyers can't stipulate value for layers they never see itemized, and every assembled-from-vendors sum lands at or above the install price (home services ~$22–77K, jewelry ~$25–75K, industrial ~$110–330K, vs from-$30K). But the adversarial critique found the dental pattern is **not a clean re-skin**: two elements genuinely break on a literal port (the Day-91 bridge dies on the sell-product clock, and the dental rate letter's "$2,500/mo" line revived the anchor D12 retired — now fixed), and five more survive only with discipline rules attached. The pattern ships as a **proposal-layer tool** (rate letter / SOW / call sheet) governed by the rules in §1; pages keep the architecture's §9 matrix untouched. Defense is a *function* (protect what you have from erosion), not dental's compliance list: home services leads with TCPA-consent texting and FTC-compliant review velocity, jewelry with ADA (retail e-commerce is 77% of all web-accessibility suits) and the transferable-vs-liquidation binary, industrial with the Amazon toll, customer concentration, and owner-dependence — and **no vertical under ~$26M revenue may be sold "CCPA protection," because the law doesn't apply to them.**

## 1. The binding rules (from the adversarial critique — these are what make the pattern safe)

The critique's verdicts: 5 SAFE-WITH-MITIGATION, 2 BREAKS. The mitigations below are adopted as rules; the breaks are rebuilt in §3.

| # | Rule | Why (critique finding) |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The fee is never itemized.** The ledger prices what each layer costs *elsewhere*; our install carries one number. Extend D10's anti-line-item rule from the retainer to the install. | Parts-pricing and Weiss condition-pricing coexist only if parts stay a subordinate **value floor** — never the number the fee is derived from or negotiated against. |
| R2 | **The ledger sells the sum, pre-empts the cherry-pick.** Frame: "assembled from 4–5 vendors, nobody accountable, no guarantee anywhere in the pile." Substrate layers (the website = the surface the attribution runs on) are scoped **jointly-delivered-only**, not à la carte. Expect "we already have a web agency" in consumer and "we have web/ERP staff" in industrial — answer it in the ledger's own copy. | Itemizing hands a sophisticated buyer a shopping list; the severability temptation is HIGHER in the ported verticals than in dental. |
| R3 | **Sequence: condition first, parts second.** The stipulated $500K–$1M+ condition is the dominant anchor; the ledger appears after it as "and even piecemeal, the parts run $X–Y." Never headline the parts sum. | A $55–120K parts anchor is weaker than the condition anchor; letting the buyer fixate on it collapses the fee ceiling from 10:1-on-condition to bulk-discount-on-parts. |
| R4 | **The value stack never migrates to a page.** Proposal artifacts only, personalized 1:1, never downloadable/public/linked. It prints sourced EV/valuation multiples (permitted, GATE:HUMAN) — never the `lib/stats.ts` six. Multiples appear only AFTER value is stipulated. | The page/proposal wall is doctrinally clean (§9 of the architecture already draws it) but erodes culturally; make it a written rule. |
| R5 | **Defense = function, native content per vertical.** Offense grows; defense protects what exists from erosion. Fill it only with the vertical's own concrete erosion — never port dental's compliance list. If a vertical's defense can't name an erosion the offense doesn't already cover, drop the face rather than pad it. On industrial (anti-jargon buyer), consider demoting the literal "offense/defense" header — keep the function, lose the metaphor. | Legal-liability defense is thin for B2B; a padded face is "gold trim the buyer smells." |
| R6 | **The ladder's rationale stays internal, and is restated even internally as "10% of your stipulated value."** Never "a number that makes $45K look small." Tier 3 real, capacity-true, value-gated (absent below ~$1.5–2M stipulated). | The wide ladder is the floor's own rule extended — it stays "no games" only while that's literally true. |
| R7 | **Bought-alone figures go through claims discipline.** GATE:HUMAN per row; OPENED at the URL before printing; floors ("from $X") where market spread makes a range misleading (industrial site + catalog layers, editorial as rate-based). Tie to our own published bands where possible. | "Prevailing market ranges" with informal sourcing is the drip-pricing pattern this buyer is primed to detect. |
| R8 | **No bare monthly figure on any install** (D12 discipline). The Beautiful Smiles "$2,500/mo" line is removed (sheet v5 + rate letter, fixed 2026-07-07). Affordability = staged billing 50/25/25; any extended schedule stays behind the [DECIDE] gate, priced as true year-one all-in, and is a "payment schedule," never "financing." | The line revived the exact anchor D12 retired, contradicted its own gate, and understated year-one all-in at the commitment moment. |
| R9 | **Statutory penalties stay calm.** One penalty figure per artifact maximum, adjacent to the not-legal-advice fineprint; never lead with penalty math. Delivery promises created by itemization (article counts, SKU counts) are sized to what one operator ships inside the audited window — an itemized line is a contractual line. | Fear-selling collides with the calm-voice rule; visible value means visible under-delivery. |

**Two faces (native content):** Offense = every call answered, every quote chased, found for the trade's searches, past customers worked, proven on the report, the shop worth more at diligence. Defense = TCPA-consent texting (lead), FTC-compliant review velocity, cooling-off/contract hygiene (anti-storm-chaser trust line), license display, strictest-state recording, the dispute-proof call log (double weight in insurance-restoration disputes).

For home services, recommend the same **foundation-shaped proposal as the default presentation**, with the same levers that protected hours on Beautiful Smiles: content at ~10/month across the install, growth-past-N on the retainer; site scoped at signature; anything slow-curve labeled proving-window/retainer work (D5's own rule). The engine-only install remains the fallback shape for shops that already have a real site.

**Two faces:** Offense = found first (site + library + maps), answered in seconds, carts and quotes chased, the book worked (band/anniversary pipeline), proven monthly. Defense = **ADA e-commerce (print hard — 77% of suits, $5–20K settlements)**, FTC-compliant review velocity, TCPA-consented texting of the book, "nothing held hostage" (site, list, content, data — theirs), and the exit-binary from the spec's §2.3 (demand that transfers vs a going-out-of-business sale) — that last one is the *strongest* defense line for the 60+ owner and needs no statute.

**The bridge, rebuilt (critique BREAK #1):** no "Day 91," no guarantee closer, "we" voice. The honest sell-product version — **"What's yours at install-complete / what the cylinders keep in motion"**: left column = the site, the library, the sequences, the dashboard, artifacts in editable form, transfer terms in the SOW; right column = cylinders added as they pay for themselves, each month a re-decision, leave on 90 days' notice and everything goes with you. The decision moment is named honestly: "the monthly report re-earns the retainer or it doesn't — and the report is yours either way." Work shown week 4 carries the early-evidence job the guarantee carries on book-jobs.

### 3.3 Industrial — ADOPT with reinterpretation (the strongest ledger; the quietest framing)

**The ledger is the best argument in the whole pass** — assembled-from-vendors runs **~$110,000–$330,000+**, 3–10× the install floor:

|---|---|---|

| AI-search/SEO foundation | $10,000–$30,000 one-time (technical audit comp; B2B SEO retainers run $7–15K/mo — one quarter retained exceeds our whole SEO slice). Caveat: no published GEO-specific price exists; this comp *understates* novelty | OPENED (Directive 2026) |
| Measurement layer (two lines, quote-log) | $7,500–$15,000 (near-exact GA4 mid-market comp) | OPENED (Coders.dev) |
| Editorial foundation | rate-based only: $0.50–$1.00/word for spec/technical content — no packaged market comp exists; never print a hard dollar range (R7) | SEARCH |
| Outbound program setup | $1,500–$5,000 setup (ongoing mgmt $2–7.5K/mo as the recurring-alternative contrast) | OPENED (Reachoutly) |
| **Σ assembled** | **~$110,000 (lean) to $330,000+ (loaded)** — vs the from-$30K install | derived |

Floors, not ranges, on the two dominant layers (site, catalog) — the market spread is 3–5× and a range would imply false precision.

**The two functions, without the sports metaphor (R5):** this buyer's page already speaks "Get found. Win the quote. Keep the account." — so the frame lands as **"grow it / keep it"** or simply two labeled groups, not "offense/defense." Grow = new RFQs, quotes closed faster, cited in the AI answer. Keep = the erosion that's already priced in dollars: the Amazon toll (12% of every migrated million, every year), house accounts leaking to whoever emailed first, customer concentration cutting the multiple 20–35%, demand that dies with the owner's phone habits. Every item is banked in the industrial spec (C1/C2, C5, F7, §2.6); nothing is new — it's assembled into one visible structure for the SOW.

**The bridge:** the spec's objection #5 ("What do we actually own if this ends?") elevated into the two-column transfer block on the SOW — what transfers on 90 days' notice (everything the fee built) vs what the cylinders keep in motion (the operating: reading the two lines, re-aiming the weakest). Monthly re-decision + 90-day exit is the pressure symmetric to book-jobs' guarantee. Tier 3 = FGO stated with its annual figure on the SOW ($20K/mo ≈ $240K/yr) so the install anchors against the real top of the ladder that already exists.
