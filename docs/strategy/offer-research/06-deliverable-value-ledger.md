### The three pitch corrections — binding

**1. The deadline did not pass.** It moved. Interim final rule 91 FR 25496, effective May 7, 2026, pushed the WCAG 2.1 AA dates to May 11, 2027 (15+ employees) and May 10, 2028 (under 15). Four days before the old date would have hit. Any copy saying "the federal deadline has passed" is wrong and a prospect can disprove it in one search.

**2. §504 does not cover everyone.** HHS's own analysis puts it at about 43% of dentists. The trigger is billing Medicaid or CHIP. Cash-pay and private-insurance-only practices are not bound by this rule. Present it scoped: *if you bill Medicaid or CHIP, this applies to you.* Never universal.

**3. Lead with Title III and the Florida numbers.** Those apply to every practice and rest on a Supreme Court case and a law firm's PACER analysis: 42 U.S.C. §12181(7)(F) plus *Bragdon v. Abbott*, 524 U.S. 624 (1998), a Title III case against a dental office. Honesty rider: *Bragdon* settled that dental offices are public accommodations. It was a service-discrimination case, not a website case. Never imply the Court ruled on websites. Then the filings: 961 federal web-accessibility suits in Florida in 2025, second nationally, nearly double 2024's 470 (Seyfarth Shaw, March 2026).

## 3. The kill list — binding

These may never appear in any Sale Solution copy, call, or email. Several of them circulate widely in AI-written vendor blogs, which means they will be offered to us again by a tool, a contractor, or a prospect's last agency. The answer is no each time.

1. **"67% of missed callers immediately call a competitor."** No study, in any source class. AI-blog laundering.
2. **"78% of callers hang up on voicemail."** Same. No study behind it.
3. **"$475–$800 per empty chair-hour" and "$200–$400 per missed appointment."** No source. Compute from the practice's own hourly production instead, which is what the audit does.
4. **"The average practice has over $1,000,000 in unscheduled treatment."** Vendor claim with no method and no date, and a 20× spread against other sources. Their own unscheduled-treatment report prints the real number.
5. **"Financing lifts case acceptance 35%."** Unsourced vendor-blog claim. The financing research publisher does not publish it.
6. **IBM's $7.42M healthcare breach average, in any dental copy.** Enterprise sample. It reads as inflated to an owner, and it is. Internal context only, with that warning attached.
7. **The ANA programmatic-waste number ($22B, one dollar in four) as a local-search proxy.** It measures open-web programmatic display for 21 national advertisers. Permitted framing, no number: if the calls aren't tracked, the waste in the account is unmeasured, not zero.
8. **"80% of patients find their dentist online — ADA."** Misattributed. Untraceable to the ADA or its Health Policy Institute.
9. **The "2026" Google Business Profile statistics** (20 website clicks, 16 direction requests, 10 calls; 781 discovery / 228 direct). Laundered rewrites of BrightLocal's 2019 study, with splits that contradict the primary numbers.
10. **"Florida is 24% of accessibility suits."** Matches neither denominator. Corrected: 961 federal web-accessibility filings in 2025, second nationally, roughly 31% of federal web filings, nearly double 2024's 470 (Seyfarth Shaw, March 2026).
11. **"The federal accessibility deadline has passed."** It moved to May 11, 2027 and May 10, 2028 (91 FR 25496). Saying otherwise is disprovable in one search.
12. **"§504 applies to every practice."** About 43% of dentists, triggered by Medicaid or CHIP billing (HHS Regulatory Impact Analysis). Scope it or drop it.
13. **"$15,000 with patient-form integrations."** Unverifiable. Keep the idea qualitative: more when patient forms are in scope.
14. **"$3,000–$10,000 for rushed post-settlement remediation."** No source at all.

Not killed, but labeled: the **$5,000–$20,000 accessibility settlement band** carries a mandatory tag every time it appears — commonly cited by remediation vendors, no primary dataset. Same for every "market range" for consulting, retainers, and fractional work in §2: those come from firms selling the service.

## 6. Delivery safeguard — our own ops rule

Three of the OCR dental cases in group 6 were review-reply cases: **New Vision Dental $23,000**, **Elite Dental Associates $10,000**, **Igbinadolor DMD $50,000**. Each one turned on a practice writing about a patient in a public reply.

**The rule for our delivery: the review engine never generates or sends a reply that references any patient detail.** No treatment, no visit, no date, no confirmation that the reviewer is a patient at all. Replies thank, they invite an offline conversation, and they say nothing else. This holds whether the review is glowing or hostile, and it holds for drafts a human will approve later.

**ADD TO ONBOARDING CHECKLIST.** It belongs with the BAA gate, not in a style guide.
