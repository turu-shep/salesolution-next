## The thesis

**One operator runs the whole flow: bring the right buyers in, convert the demand you already have, keep and re-sell the customers you already won.**

Most vendors sell one slice — an ads guy who optimizes clicks, an SEO guy who sends a rankings report, a chatbot, a reviews tool. Each proves *their slice fired*; none owns whether you made money. The leak isn't in any single slice — it's in the **seams** between them: the lead the ads guy bought, that nobody called back, on a page the SEO guy never built to convert. So the unit of work is the **flow**, not the tactic.

- **Ads are one input into Bring**, run on the client's own account at cost, zero markup. Ads are fuel; the engine is everything that turns demand into booked, paid, repeat revenue.
- **Prove** is the measurement layer under all three pillars: two revenue lines (media-driven vs. system-driven) in the client's own dashboard.
- **Same spine, two doors.** Local-service businesses get the productized **Revenue Engine** (CAPTURE → RESPOND → BOOK → RECOVER → PROVE). $5M–$75M industrial distributors/manufacturers get the consultative **services book** run by one accountable owner. (Tagline: *Revenue systems for businesses that sell parts, book jobs, and fill chairs.*)

## The three pillars (+ Prove)

### Bring — "Get more of the right work coming in, without just spending more on ads."
Demand **creation** (make buyers aware they have a problem you fix) + demand **capture** (intercept intent that already exists), across every place a buyer now looks: Google, ChatGPT / AI Overviews, Maps, marketplaces, the inbox, referrals. On a DR-10 site the play is **citation and authority, not volume SEO.**

- **Levers:** GEO/AEO (be *cited inside* AI answers — the signature play) · organic SEO on commercial/spec/part-number intent · Local SEO + Google Business Profile + map pack · editorial + original-data authority · digital PR + earned citations · paid search on high-intent (one input, judged on cost-per-booked-outcome) · vertical-scoped paid/retargeting · reply-first cold outbound · marketplaces (be named *over* Amazon) · reviews as a discovery signal.
- **Owned metric:** AI-answer citation share + cost-per-booked-outcome. Never impressions / CTR / rankings in isolation.

### Convert — "Stop leaking the leads and quotes I already paid to win."
Everything after the hand-raise: response speed, whether the call is answered, whether it books, whether the page/catalog makes it easy to say yes, whether the quote closes, whether checkout completes. **This is the engine; the leak lives here, not in the ad budget.**

- **Levers:** speed-to-lead (sub-60-second reply; industry average is 47 hours) · call answering / AI receptionist 24/7 · missed-call text-back · booking + reminders · multi-touch nurture · CRO / owned landing pages · machine-readable product/category pages + clear quote path · fast, easy-to-accept quotes + financing framing · cart recovery · proof at the decision point · scripts, call recording, front-desk scoring.
- **Owned metric:** speed-to-lead, answer rate, lead-to-appointment, show rate, quote win rate, cost-per-booked-job (not raw CPL).
- *Naming:* the canonical word is **Convert**. Build components currently say "Sell" — standardize to Convert.

### Retain — "Sell again to who already bought, win back who went quiet, chase the quotes that went cold."
Everything after the first sale: keep them, recover the lapsed, grow what each is worth. **This is where SMBs under-invest the most; the dormant database is usually the single largest pool of recoverable revenue an owner is sitting on.**

- **Levers:** quote/estimate/treatment-plan follow-up (the cold-deal recovery engine — highest-dollar lever) · reactivation/win-back of the dormant list · review/reputation engine (also feeds Bring via the map pack) · systematized referrals · recall/recurring · lifecycle email + SMS to the owned list · membership/maintenance/loyalty · cross-sell/upsell/share-of-wallet · RFM segmentation · owned-audience retargeting + abandoned-cart.
- **Owned metric:** system-attributed (recovered) revenue, reactivation revenue, close-rate-after-follow-up, review velocity, repeat rate, LTV:CAC — *the second revenue line that should clear the fee on its own.*

### Prove — the measurement spine under all three
Two revenue lines on every monthly report: **media-driven** (ad spend through the engine) vs. **system-driven** (recovered calls, follow-up, reactivation, review-driven organic). The whole proof is that the *second line alone clears the fee*, shown as plain math in the client's own dashboard — because the Revenue Engine day-90 guarantee is written against it. Underneath: call recording/transcription, dispute-proof lead log, distinct tracking-number pools (so recovered calls aren't miscredited to ads), server-side deduped conversions, funnel-stage events so you can *locate* the leak. CAC-vs-LTV frames spend; Retain pushes effective CAC toward zero. No fabricated numbers; proof slots stay empty until real cohort data exists. The industrial/services side deliberately holds a **no-guarantee** stance — kept separate, never blended with the Revenue Engine promise.

## The boundary (what keeps this from being a generic full-service agency)

The operator model *is* the wedge: one accountable owner, named verticals, published prices, a falsifiable guarantee — plus a hard scope line. **Sale Solution does NOT do:**

- Brand strategy, paid-social creative, social-community management.
- Markup on ads or reselling leads — ads run on the client's account at cost; no shared lead pool (every lead in a dispute-proof log).
- Annual lock-in — on cancel the automations switch off but the client keeps their ad account, data, and Google profile.
- Fabricated testimonials/results; no review schema until real reviews exist.
- Volume SEO — on a DR-10 site the play is citation, authority, and AI-answer presence.
- A revenue guarantee on the industrial/services side — the day-90 guarantee is the Revenue Engine's only, kept separate on purpose.
- Replace the front desk, the ads vendor, or the Google rep — every replacement fear is reframed as additive.

The authority hub (glossary + AI-search career paths) is measured on referring domains and AI citations, not leads — a deliberate non-converting asset.

## Honest risks (and why the model survives them)

1. **"Whole flow" reads like the generic agency the brand bans** → the operator wedge + the boundary list keep scope honest and narrow.
2. **Delivery surface** (one operator can't execute four verticals at scale) → productized Revenue Engine + Full Growth Ownership's two shapes (fractional GTM engineer vs. coordinated team).
3. **DR-10 credibility** ("physician, heal thyself") → the constraint *is* the strategy, stated openly; authority hub measured on citations, not leads.
4. **Two funnels / four verticals blurring** → two non-merging doors, vertical-scoped retargeting, "we" (industrial) vs "I" (Revenue Engine).
5. **Bus factor of "one operator"** → productized systems + documented playbooks; the client owns code/data/profile, so no hostage risk.
