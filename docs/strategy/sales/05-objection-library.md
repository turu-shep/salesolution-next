# Objection Library (both motions)

**What this is:** the card you find mid-call when a prospect pushes back. Hear the phrase, find the trigger, read the line. Word-for-word. Both motions. Every branch.

**How to read a card**
- **They say** — the trigger. Search the phrase you just heard.
- **Tag · Motion** — gatekeeper / brush-off / price / trust / timing / competitor / fit. RE = Revenue Engine (local-service). IND = industrial. Both = both.
- **Say** — out loud, on voice. *(stop — let them answer)* means stop talking. The silence does the work.
- **If they hold the line** — one re-ask, then the door or the send. Never a third push.
- **Why it lands** — one line, for you, not them.

**Three rules above every card**

1. **You looked before you dialed.** On RE calls, before this number rang you called their main line and timed the callback, opened their Google Business Profile, and looked at their site. So you don't ask *"what happens when you miss a call?"* — you *say* what you saw: *"I called your line at 6:40 last night, got voicemail, no callback yet."* An observed fact can't be argued. A hypothetical invites *"that doesn't happen to me."* If you didn't get to look, say so and offer to — don't bluff an observation you don't have.

2. **Never quote a price cold.** Both motions, price routes to the door. RE → the Revenue Leak Audit. IND → the Growth Call. The number is real. It's just not a cold-call number, and saying so *is* the trust signal.

3. **One rebuttal, one re-ask, then let go.** If it's still no after that, route to email and get off the phone. With a burned buyer, the second hard push is how you become the last guy. Protecting the name is worth more than forcing the booking.

**A word on the word "leak."** "Revenue Leak Audit" is the offer name — say it. But don't say *"where'd that leak go"* or *"that's the leak"* to a problem-aware owner as if it's his word. It's ours. Out loud, name the real thing: the missed call, the voicemail, the job that went to the next guy. Save "leak" for the audit name and for your own head.

## The blocks you reuse (say these verbatim — never paraphrase)

These three blocks appear across many cards. Same words every time. A prospect who hears two versions of your guarantee decides you're making it up.

**THE GUARANTEE (RE, verbatim):**
> "Here's the guarantee. If the system doesn't bring in more money than it costs you by day 90, I work for free until it does."

**THE TERMS (RE, verbatim):**
> "No 12-month contract. Ninety days to build it, three months minimum so it gets a fair shot, then month to month — leave anytime with 30 days' notice. When you leave, you keep your ad account, your data, and your Google profile. I don't hold any of it."

**THE AUDIT OFFER (RE, verbatim):**
> "Free, about 20 minutes. I show you your own numbers — missed calls, how fast you reply, your Google profile, where follow-up drops. Yours to keep whether you hire me or not."

## Section 1 — Gatekeeper

### G1. "What's this regarding?"

**Tag:** gatekeeper · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "Your main line rang out to voicemail a few minutes ago — that's actually why I'm calling. Two minutes with [Owner] about it. Is he around?"

**Say (IND):**
> "Quick one for [Owner] about how [Company]'s coming up when buyers search for parts. Is he in?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** "No problem. What's his direct email? I'll send him the two numbers I pulled and stay out of your hair." Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** Lead with the observed fact, not your mission. "I help shops stop losing jobs" pattern-matches to a pitch by word six — so it's cut. "Your line rang out to voicemail" makes them curious instead.

### G2. "He's not available." / "He's in a meeting."

**Tag:** gatekeeper · **Motion:** Both

**Say:**
> "Figured. Is mornings generally better, or should I try mid-afternoon?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they won't give a time:** "All good. Can I grab his direct email so I'm not going through the front line every time?" Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** A binary ("morning or afternoon") is easier to answer than an open schedule question, and it assumes a callback instead of asking permission to be turned away.

### G3. "Put it in an email to info@ / sales@."

**Tag:** gatekeeper · **Motion:** Both

**Say:**
> "I can do that. Honestly, that info@ box is half of what I'd ask him about — it's usually where leads go to sit. Does he have a direct email so it lands in front of him?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** "info@ works if that's easier — I'll keep it to one short note so it doesn't get buried." Then EMAIL 1 to info@.

**Why it lands:** On RE this *is* the problem — a shared inbox nobody works, which you probably saw on their site. You turn the brush-off into your own evidence without arguing.

### G4. "Are you selling something?"

**Tag:** gatekeeper · **Motion:** Both

**Say:**
> "Eventually, sure, if it's a fit. Right now I've got two numbers on [Company]'s phone and Google profile I'd rather show him than sell him on. Is he the right person?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** "No problem — his direct email and I'll send the numbers over." Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** One "yes," then pivot to proof. Three stacked hedges ("eventually, maybe, if it's a fit") read as squirming. A burned gatekeeper screens evasion, not honesty.

### G5. "That's my husband's / wife's thing — he's not here." (small-shop spouse)

**Tag:** gatekeeper / fit · **Motion:** RE (mostly)

**Say:**
> "Got it — and you probably catch half these calls yourself. When the phone rings and nobody can grab it, where's it going right now — voicemail, your cell?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** "Fair. What's the best number or email for [Owner]? I called the main line earlier and it went to voicemail — that's the thing I'd want to show him." Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** In a small trade the spouse is often the one losing the calls and the real influencer. Don't talk past them. Hand them the same fact and they'll carry it to the owner.

### G6. (Dental) Office manager answers.

**Tag:** gatekeeper · **Motion:** RE (dental)

**Say:**
> "You're probably the right person, actually. Quick one — when the front desk is checking a patient in and two calls come in at once, what happens to the second one? Voicemail, or does it ring out?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** "Makes sense. I help practices stop losing the calls that hit during chair time. Worth 20 minutes with you and the doctor to look at your numbers?" If not, get the email. Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** The office manager isn't a wall to get past — they own the phones and often steer the decision. Speak to their actual problem (the second simultaneous call), not the doctor's.

## Section 2 — Reflex brush-offs

### B1. "Not interested." (before they've heard anything)

**Tag:** brush-off · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "Fair — you don't know what it is yet. Ten seconds and you can hang up: I called your line last night at 6:40 and got voicemail. No callback yet. If that was a customer, he's calling the next roofer on the list right now."

*(stop — let them answer)*

- If *"we catch those / I check voicemail":* "Good — how fast does the callback usually go out? Past about five minutes, most people have already called the next name on the list."
- If *"we don't miss calls":* "Then you're ahead of most. The one I hit last night went to voicemail though — want me to send you what I saw, and you tell me if it's a fluke?"

**Say (IND):**
> "Fair, you don't know me. Ten seconds — I asked Google's AI this morning who sells [category] in [region]. Want me to tell you whether it named you, and hang up either way?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** "No worries — I'll send you the one thing I saw and get out of your hair." Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** The reflex "no" is to the interruption, not to an offer you haven't made. You replace the interruption with one true, specific fact about *their* business. Curiosity beats the reflex. Note: don't say "leak" out loud here — say the missed call and the next roofer, which is the picture in his head already.

> **Honesty gate on B1 (IND):** only say "I asked the AI" if you *actually ran it* and have the result in front of you. If they say "show me" and you bluffed, you're the last guy. Run the query before you dial, or change the opener.

### B2. "Just email me." / "Send me some info."

**Tag:** brush-off · **Motion:** Both

**Say:**
> "I will. So I send the right thing and not a generic deck — when a call comes in and you can't pick up, where's it going right now?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

- If they give you an answer (voicemail / cell / receptionist): "Got it. I'll send you the two numbers I already pulled on that. Best email — your direct, or is info@ easier?" *(stop — let them answer)*
- If *"we don't miss calls" / "it's covered":* "Then you're in good shape. I'll send you what I saw last night anyway — one minute to read — and you tell me if it's nothing." Get the email.

**If they hold the line:** Send it. Keep it to EMAIL 1 — one observation, no brochure, the CTA is a question. Don't dump a deck.

**Why it lands:** "Just email me" is the polite end of a call. You agree instantly so there's no friction, then earn one answer that makes the email land warm. The "it's covered" branch keeps you from going silent when they don't play along.

### B3. "We're not looking for anything right now."

**Tag:** brush-off / timing · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "Makes sense — most shops aren't *looking*, the missed calls just sit in the background. I'm not asking you to buy anything. I found one specific thing on your end this morning. Can I tell you what it was, and you decide if it's worth 20 minutes later?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**Say (IND):**
> "Right, and I'm not pitching a project. The way buyers find parts changed, and most owners can't see it happen. When's the last time a new buyer told you they found you through Google or an AI search?"

*(stop — let them answer)*
> "Right — that channel moved, and most catalogs got skipped without the owner ever knowing. That's the thing I'd check."

**If they hold the line:** EMAIL 1. Mark warm-later, not dead.

**Why it lands:** Separate "not buying today" from "not worth knowing about." They've only refused the first. The IND question survives "I don't track that" — it asks what they'd remember, not a metric they may not have.

## Section 3 — "We already have someone"

### C1. "We already have a marketing guy / an agency."

**Tag:** competitor · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "Good — you should. I'm not here to replace your ads guy. Keep him. He brings the calls in. I make sure the calls that come in actually book. Different job. The leads he sends you — do they get a text back in under a minute, or do they sit?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

- If *"I don't run ads":* "Even simpler, then. It's your own calls and referrals we're talking about — and I called your line last night, it went to voicemail. Those are the calls going to the next guy."

**Say (IND):**
> "Glad to hear it. Has he shown you the actual AI answer for [category] — where you land when a buyer asks ChatGPT who sells this part?"

*(stop — let them answer)*
> "If he has and you're named, you're set and I'll leave you alone. If he hasn't, that's the new part, and it's where most catalogs are getting skipped."

**If they hold the line:** "Send me his last report — I'll tell you straight if there's a gap, no charge." Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** Burned owners brace for "fire your guy." You say "keep him," which kills the fight, then carve out the slice he isn't covering. On IND, ask the verifying question *first* — don't hand them the exit before they've shown they can't answer it.

### C2. "My nephew / kid / someone in-house handles it."

**Tag:** competitor / fit · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "That's fine for a lot of it. Here's the one thing a part-timer can't do: answer the phone and text a customer back at 8pm on a Saturday. I called your line after hours last night and got voicemail — that's the exact gap. Who's catching those?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**Say (IND):**
> "No knock on them. The question is whether they can rebuild your product pages so the AI reads your catalog and names you over the manufacturer. That's a specific job. Has anybody checked whether ChatGPT names you right now?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** RE → THE AUDIT OFFER. IND → "I'll run your name through the AI and send you what comes back." Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** Don't insult the nephew — the owner will defend him. Respect him and isolate the one task that needs a system or a specialist, tied to something you actually observed.

### C3. "We get plenty of word-of-mouth / referrals." (RE)

**Tag:** competitor / brush-off · **Motion:** RE

**Say:**
> "Best kind of work there is — keep it. But word-of-mouth still rings your phone. I called that phone last night and got voicemail. A referral that hits voicemail doesn't always wait around — he's got the next guy's number too. Stop dropping those calls and you close more of the work you're already winning."

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** THE AUDIT OFFER — "I'll pull your missed-call count, it's yours either way." Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** Agree referrals are gold, then show the problem hits *every* call, not just paid ones — and you've got the voicemail to prove it's real for them.

## Section 4 — Price

### P1. "What does it cost?" / "Just give me a number."

**Tag:** price · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "I won't make up a number on the phone — it depends on your volume and your area, and you'd know I was guessing. Here's the real version." → THE AUDIT OFFER → "and the price comes out of what's actually being missed, in writing, same day. Want it?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**Say (IND):**
> "I'm not going to quote you cold — it'd be a made-up number and you'd know it. The Growth Call scopes what's actually wrong first. Thirty minutes, I look at where you're getting skipped, then I tell you what it'd take. No number before I've looked. That's the deal."

**If they hold the line:** RE → the Revenue Leak Audit booking link. IND → the Growth Call, or the written diagnostic at /unlock-growth-audit/ as the secondary door.

**Why it lands:** Refusing to invent a number *is* the trust signal to a burned buyer. You swap "how much" for "let's find what you're missing first," which is a better hook anyway.

### P2. "Ballpark it. Hundreds or thousands?"

**Tag:** price · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "It's a monthly system, not a one-time fee, and it's built to bring in more than it costs. But I'd be guessing at the real number till I see your call volume. Twenty minutes on the audit and you've got a figure tied to your actual numbers, not my ballpark. Fair?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**Say (IND):**
> "Depends how much is broken — a catalog cleanup is one thing, running the whole channel is another. That's what the call sorts out. I'd rather scope it than throw you a range that's useless."

**If they hold the line:** RE → THE GUARANTEE, then the audit link. IND → the Growth Call.

**Why it lands:** Give them the *shape* (monthly, guarantee-backed) without a fake number, then trade the ballpark for a real one.

### P3. "That's probably out of our budget." (no number quoted yet)

**Tag:** price / fit · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "You might be right — but you're guessing at a number I haven't given you. Flip it: if the audit shows even a couple of missed calls a week were real jobs, that answers the budget question on its own. If it doesn't, I'll tell you, and we don't work together. Twenty minutes to find out?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**Say (IND):**
> "Could be. The Growth Call's free, and if what it'd cost doesn't come back to you in quotes, I'll say so on the call and we leave it there."

**If they hold the line:** RE → the audit. IND → the Growth Call or written diagnostic.

**Why it lands:** They've objected to a price you never said. Don't argue it — point out they're guessing, and offer to replace the guess with their own real number. No invented counts.

## Section 5 — Trust / burned

### T1. "I've been burned by agencies before."

**Tag:** trust · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "Then you'll hate half of what's out there, and you should. So here's what I *don't* do. No markup on your ads. I don't resell your leads to three other roofers."

*(stop — let it land, then:)* → THE TERMS → THE GUARANTEE

> "If any of that sounds like the last guy, tell me and I'll let you go."

**Say (IND):**
> "Makes sense. A lot of this got sold on 'guaranteed rankings' and a monthly report nobody read. I don't guarantee rankings — nobody honestly can. What I'll do on the call is show you exactly where you're getting passed over in the AI answers, and what fixing it looks like. If it's vague, you walk."

**If they hold the line:** EMAIL 1 — plain text, one true observation, no deck. Burned buyers trust restraint.

**Why it lands:** Agree with the distrust instead of fighting it, then list concrete things you refuse to do. Specifics about your own limits read as honest. Promises read as the last guy. Don't read THE TERMS and THE GUARANTEE back to back as one breath — pause after "three other roofers" so the *don't*-do list lands before you talk terms. Use the "tell me and I'll let you go" exit once across a call, not as a tic.

### T2. "How do I know you're any different?"

**Tag:** trust · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "You don't yet — and I'm not going to ask you to take my word." → THE AUDIT OFFER → "If a pitch came before the proof, you'd be right to be suspicious. So the proof comes first."

**Say (IND):**
> "Fair. The difference is I'll show you the actual AI answer for your category before I ask for anything. If it names a competitor instead of you, that's not my opinion — it's on your screen. We start from what's true."

**If they hold the line:** Send the thing you pulled — the two numbers, or the AI result. Then the door.

**Why it lands:** "You don't know yet" is honest and unexpected. Then you replace *trust me* with *see for yourself*, which is the whole positioning.

### T3. "Send me references. Who else have you worked with?"

**Tag:** trust · **Motion:** Both

**Say:**
> "Totally fair to want them — and I'll get you references after this call. The reason I lead with your own numbers is they're about *you*, not somebody else's shop. If the math on your business doesn't make the case, no reference will. So let me show you your own numbers first — then if you still want references, you've got them."

*(stop — let them answer)*
> *(Do NOT name a client or quote a case-study number on a cold call.)*

**If they hold the line:** Book the audit/call. After the call, send approved references through the proper channel.

**Why it lands:** Don't dodge the ask and don't reframe it away — wanting references is a legitimate procurement reflex. Acknowledge it, then reprioritize: their own data is more persuasive, and it keeps you off the thin ice of name-dropping you can't verify.

> **Hard rule for this section:** never invent a client name, a stat, or a result. "Northern Hydraulics" and all client names are off-limits in scripts. If they push for proof you can't source live, the proof is their own numbers. Everything else is a [VERIFY] follow-up after the call.

## Section 6 — Timing

### TM1. "Now's a bad time." / "I'm slammed."

**Tag:** timing · **Motion:** Both

**Say:**
> "I bet. Two ways to go: I give you the one number I pulled in 30 seconds right now, or you name a better time and I call back then. Which is easier?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** Get the callback time *and* the direct email. EMAIL 1 in between.

**Why it lands:** "Bad time" is usually true and a soft no. You give them an easy exit *and* a tiny hook, so the call doesn't just die. Use this two-option move once per prospect, not on every timing card.

### TM2. "Call me back next quarter / after the season."

**Tag:** timing · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "I'll put it in the calendar. One thing before I go — during your busy season, is the phone ringing more than usual, or less?"

*(stop — let them answer)*
> "Right — more. So that's when the most calls are slipping past, when you've got the least time to catch them. Let me just send you your missed-call number today, and we talk for real in [month]."

**Say (IND):**
> "Will do. Quick thing — the AI-answer work isn't a switch you flip, it takes a few months to move. So if we wait till [later], we're really starting then. No pressure today. Let me send you where you stand now, so it's ready when you are."

**If they hold the line:** Book the callback. Send the observation now. Tag for nurture.

**Why it lands:** Honor the no, then plant one honest reason the cost is real now. On RE, make *them* say "more" instead of you asserting it — same truth, no squeeze. No fake deadline, ever — that blows your credibility with this exact buyer.

## Section 7 — "Doing fine / too good to be true"

### F1. "We're doing fine." / "We're busy enough."

**Tag:** brush-off / fit · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "Good — that's the right time to fix this, when you're not desperate. Here's the thing about busy: busy is exactly when calls get missed, because you're on a job and the phone's ringing. I called your line during business hours yesterday and got voicemail. Want me to pull how many of those happened last month? It's free."

*(stop — let them answer)*

**Say (IND):**
> "Glad to hear it. Most owners I talk to were doing fine too — right up until the quotes got quieter and they couldn't say why. Not saying that's you. Worth 30 seconds to check whether the AI names you or a competitor for your parts. Want me to look while we're on?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** Send the observation. Mark warm-later.

**Why it lands:** "Fine" is a feeling. The missed-call count and the AI answer are facts. Offer to swap one for the other — and you've already got the voicemail to make it real.

### F2. "Sounds too good to be true." / "Does this even work?"

**Tag:** trust · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE, roofing):**
> "Good instinct — if someone promised me guaranteed jobs I'd hang up too. So here's the boring version. I don't promise leads. The calls you already get and the quotes you already send go to the next guy when nobody answers fast enough — I fix that. The proof isn't my chart. It's your dashboard, in plain math." → THE GUARANTEE → "That's the whole thing. No magic. Just a system that answers the phone faster than you can while you're on a roof."

**Say (RE, dental):** same setup, ending:
> "...just a system that picks up the calls your front desk can't get to when they're checking a patient in."

**Say (IND):**
> "I get the skepticism — there's a lot of snake oil in this corner. I won't tell you I can guarantee a ranking; nobody can. What I *can* do is show you today whether the AI names you or skips you, and why. If it skips you, I can tell you the three reasons, and they're fixable. That's it. No magic."

**If they hold the line:** RE → the audit (proof on their numbers). IND → run their name through the AI live or in follow-up.

**Why it lands:** Side with the skepticism, then *lower* the claim until it sounds true. Boring and specific beats exciting and vague for this buyer. The day-90 guarantee makes "too good" testable.

## Section 8 — Fit / size

### S1. "We're too small for this." / "We're not Caterpillar." (IND)

**Tag:** fit · **Motion:** IND

**Say:**
> "You're probably not Caterpillar, and you don't need to be — this is built for owners between five and seventy-five million, not the giants. So I'm not wasting your time: roughly how many people on your sales and counter side?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

- If *"why do you ask / I'm not telling you my revenue":* "Fair — I'm only checking you're past about five million, where this starts to pay back. North of that, you're exactly who it's for. Under it, I'll tell you straight it's too early."

**If they hold the line:** In range → the Growth Call. Under $5M → graceful exit (S3), not a pitch.

**Why it lands:** Naming the band tells them where they fit, and the honest disqualifier makes the qualifier credible. Asking headcount is less intrusive cold than asking revenue outright — and gives you the same read.

### S2. "We're a small shop, this is overkill." (RE)

**Tag:** fit · **Motion:** RE

**Say:**
> "Small shops are who this is *for*. A big outfit has a front desk that answers every call. You're on the roof — you *are* the front desk, and you can't be in two places. I called your line yesterday and got voicemail, which is the proof. The system's the receptionist you can't afford to hire yet. How many calls do you figure you miss in a week?"

*(stop — let them answer)*

**Say (RE, dental small practice):**
> "Small practices lose the most, because your one front-desk person can't check in a patient and answer two ringing lines at once. I called yours yesterday during business hours and got voicemail. That's the gap."

**If they hold the line:** THE AUDIT OFFER — the missed-call count proves it.

**Why it lands:** Flip "too small" into "exactly the right size." A solo or small operator has the worst phone coverage, so the problem is *bigger*, not smaller — and you observed it.

### S3. Under $5M / clearly out of fit (IND) — graceful disqualify

**Tag:** fit · **Motion:** IND

**Say:**
> "Straight with you — what I do starts to pay back north of about five million in revenue. Below that, the math doesn't come back fast enough to be worth your cash, so I'm not going to pitch you. Two things that'll actually help you right now, free: fill out your Google Business Profile completely, and get your top products onto your site with real specs and part numbers. Do those, and call me when you're bigger. No hard feelings."

**If they hold the line:** Nothing to sell. Optionally one line: "want me to email you that two-item checklist?" Don't book them.

**Why it lands:** A clean no builds more reputation than a stretched yes. A small owner you treated straight refers the bigger ones.

## Section 9 — Revenue Engine specials

### RE1. "I already have an answering service / a receptionist."

**Tag:** competitor / fit · **Motion:** RE

**Say:**
> "Good — keep them. Two questions. Do they answer at 8pm on a Saturday, or just business hours? And when they take a message, who texts that customer back, and how fast? I called your line after hours last night and got voicemail — so something's getting past them. An answering service takes a message. This texts back in seconds, any hour, and walks the customer toward booking. They work together."

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** THE AUDIT OFFER — measure the after-hours and follow-up gap specifically.

**Why it lands:** Don't trash their service — name the two things it doesn't do: after-hours, and fast text-back. That's where the money goes, and you've got an after-hours voicemail to prove it. Say "walks them toward booking," not "books the job" — don't overclaim that the booking is automatic.

### RE2. "I'm literally on a roof right now." / "I'm with a patient."

**Tag:** timing · **Motion:** RE

**Say:**
> "Then you just proved my whole point, and I'll get off the phone. *That's* the problem — right now, while you're up there, your phone's ringing and somebody's getting voicemail. That's the exact call this fixes. Text me a good time at this number and I'll send you how many of those you're missing. Go work — talk soon."

**If they hold the line:** Text the observation. One line. Then EMAIL 1.

**Why it lands:** The interruption itself is the demonstration — you couldn't script better proof than them being unreachable mid-call. Respect them and exit fast; that builds the goodwill that gets the callback.

### RE3. "Is this HIPAA-compliant?" (dental)

**Tag:** trust / fit · **Motion:** RE (dental)

**Say (only if confirmed true today — see gate below):**

## Section 11 — Compliance & cold-call hygiene (every motion)

### H1. "Where did you get my number?" / "How'd you get this?"

**Tag:** trust / hygiene · **Motion:** Both

**Say:**
> "Straight answer — your number's listed publicly for [Company], on your site and your Google profile. I'm not working off a bought list. I call owners directly when I see something worth a heads-up, and yours had one."

*(stop — let them answer)*

**If they hold the line:** Don't argue it. "Fair to ask — I'll send the one thing I found by email instead and leave it with you." Then EMAIL 1, or close out if they decline.

**Why it lands:** The question is testing whether you're a spammer. A specific, true source ("your public listing, not a bought list") separates you from the robocall they expected.

### H2. "Take me off your list." / "Stop calling." / "Do not call me again."

**Tag:** compliance · **Motion:** Both — **non-negotiable**

**Say:**
> "Done. I'll take you off right now and you won't hear from me again. Sorry to bother you — have a good one."

**Then:** Hang up. Log the number to your internal do-not-call list immediately. No second pitch, no "before you go," no email. A stop request ends every motion.

**Why it lands:** This isn't persuasion — it's the law and your reputation. The fastest, cleanest exit is the only right answer. One more word here is how you earn a complaint.

### H3. You reach the OWNER's voicemail (what you say when *you* get the beep)

**Tag:** hygiene · **Motion:** Both — leave one, max two over a week

**Say (RE):**
> "Hi [Owner], it's Artur. I called your main line earlier and it went to voicemail — that's actually why I'm calling. I pulled a couple of quick numbers on your phone and your Google profile that you'll want to see. No pitch — I'll text them to this number, and you tell me if they're worth 20 minutes. Thanks."

**Say (IND):**
> "Hi [Owner], it's Artur. I asked Google's AI this morning who sells [category] in [region], and I want to show you what came back about [Company]. No pitch, takes a minute. I'll follow up by email — reply if you want the screenshot. Thanks."

**Then:** Send EMAIL 1 the same day. The voicemail and the email reference the same observation.

**Why it lands:** Short, specific, one reason, one easy next step. A voicemail that names a real thing about their business gets a callback. A generic "I'd love to connect" gets deleted.

### H4. (After the email) "I saw your email. Not interested."

**Tag:** brush-off / closeout · **Motion:** Both

**Say (RE):**
> "No problem at all — appreciate you saying so straight. Last thing and I'll leave it: the voicemail I flagged was real, so if the phone ever feels quieter than it should, that's the first place I'd look. Either way, I won't keep calling. Take care."

**Say (IND):**
> "No problem at all — appreciate you saying so straight. Last thing and I'll leave it: the AI named [competitor / nobody], not you, for [category] today. So if the quotes ever feel quieter than they should, that's the first place I'd look. Either way, I won't keep calling. Take care."

**Then:** Mark closed-polite. No further dials. If they ever reply later, restart warm.

**Why it lands:** They engaged enough to read it, so respect that with a clean exit that leaves one useful idea behind. Pushing here converts a soft no into a complaint — and a clean goodbye keeps the door open for a later inbound. The IND tail uses the AI result you actually pulled, not the RE voicemail line — keep the observation true to the motion.

## Quick reference — which door, every time

- **RE warms up →** book the **Revenue Leak Audit** (free ~20 min; their numbers to keep).
- **RE not ready →** send the observation you pulled (voicemail result / missed-call count / Google profile) as EMAIL 1, reply-first. Mark warm-later.
- **IND qualified ($5M+) and warm →** book the **Growth Call**.
