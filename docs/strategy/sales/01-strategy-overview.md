# Cold-Call Strategy & Operating Model

*Top of the cold-call playbook. Read this before any other module. It sets which call you're on, the only thing the call is for, and how to run a real dialing day without burning out or mixing up the two businesses.*

---

## The one rule

**Book the next step. That's the whole job.**

- Revenue Engine prospect → book a **Revenue Leak Audit**.
- Industrial prospect → book a **Growth Call**.

You're not selling on this call. You're not quoting a price on this call. You're getting 20 minutes on the calendar.

Say it before you dial: *I'm booking, not closing.*

Why it's the rule:

- **A price quoted cold kills the deal.** On both sides the number depends on what you find. Revenue Engine pricing turns on trade, location, and scope, and it lands in the audit, in writing, the same day. Industrial scope turns on what's broken. Throw a number into a vacuum and you anchor wrong, you sound like every agency that's called them, and you lose the room.
- **The audit is the product on a cold call.** What you hand a Revenue Engine prospect is *their own leak* — the call you watched go to voicemail, the slow text-back, the dead spot on their Google profile. They keep those numbers whether they hire you or not. You can't deliver that in 90 seconds on a dial. You deliver it in the 20-minute audit. So the dial exists to book the audit.
- **A burned buyer trusts a small ask.** "Give me your card" on a cold call sounds like a scam to this buyer. "Let me show you your own numbers, free, you keep them" doesn't.

If a call's going well and you feel the urge to pitch the whole thing, stop. Book the next step. The next step is where you pitch.

## Two motions, two lists

You're running two businesses through one phone. Different buyer, different problem, different CTA, different list. Keep them on separate lists in Apollo and never let a script from one bleed into the other.

| | **Revenue Engine** | **Industrial** |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Local-service owner: roofer, HVAC, plumber, electrician, dental-practice owner | Owner / president of a $5M–$75M distributor or manufacturer |
| **What they feel** | "Phone rings while I'm on a roof or with a patient. I pay for leads nobody calls back. I quote a job and never hear a word." | "Phone's quieter, fewer quotes coming in. We slipped on Google. Amazon and the manufacturers are going direct." |
| **Their words** | jobs, calls, estimates, booked, the front desk | quotes, RFQs, counter sales, line card, our reps, our parts |
| **What I sell** | A system that answers every call, texts back in seconds, books the job, chases cold quotes, and proves the revenue | Get found when buyers ask AI; stop leaking the quotes they already get |
| **The CTA** | **Revenue Leak Audit** — free, ~20 min, they keep the numbers | **Growth Call** (the written diagnostic at /unlock-growth-audit/ is the backup door) |
| **Voice** | First-person "I." Owner to owner. No hype, they can smell it. | First-person "I" / "we." Owner to owner. Revenue, not metrics. |
| **The unlock** | I tested their phone and Google profile before dialing. The opener names the real leak I found. | I checked whether ChatGPT and Google's AI name them or a competitor. |

**Why they can't share a list:**

- A roofer and a distributor president don't have the same problem, don't use the same words, and don't book the same call. A script that lands on one sounds insane to the other.
- The Revenue Engine buyer is **problem-aware, not solution-aware** — they feel the bleed but have no name for the fix. You lead with the leak. The industrial owner names their own villain (Amazon, going direct, slipped on Google), so you meet them there.
- Mix the lists and you'll open the wrong way, use the wrong words, and pitch the wrong CTA. One wrong word and a burned buyer is gone.

**The fork when a contact is borderline:** do they sell parts or jobs to other businesses, or service walk-in and residential customers? Sell-to-business with a line card and RFQs → Industrial. Service homes, patients, or local jobs → Revenue Engine. A 4-location dental group still services patients; it's Revenue Engine. A distributor owner who also owns rental property is still Industrial; you sell to the distributor.

**The $5M floor on the industrial side is real.** Below it, disqualify gracefully and move on. Don't downgrade them into a Revenue Engine pitch on the same call. If they turn out to be an actual local-service business, that's a different list on a different day, not a pivot mid-call.

Tag every contact with its motion before it gets dialed. If you can't tell which motion a contact is, it isn't ready to dial.

## The pre-call check

Two minutes per contact, done before the block, not during it. The check is what makes the opener land. A generic opener gets hung up on. A specific one — *"I tried your main line and got voicemail"* — gets a conversation.

### Revenue Engine — find the leak before you dial

This is the whole edge. Before you dial a local-service prospect, find the real leak. The call then demonstrates the exact problem we sell against. No other script has this.

1. **Call their main line first.** Voicemail? How many rings? If a human picks up, don't hang up on them — that's its own data point, they answer live, note it and move on. If it goes to voicemail, note it and time whether anyone ever calls back. In my experience most don't.
2. **Pull up their Google Business Profile.** Review count, last time they replied to one, hours, photos, whether the listed number even works. Note one specific thing.
3. **Open their website on your phone.** Does it load fast? Is there a quick way to reach a human — a text button, a form that actually replies? Note the gap.

Write one line of the real leak at the top of the contact. That line feeds your opener.

You're not accusing them of anything. You're showing them a number they didn't know they had. That's the audit in miniature.

### Industrial — the AI-answer check

1. **Ask ChatGPT or Google's AI a buyer question in their category** — "best supplier for [their parts] in [region]," or a part-number / cross-reference query. Does the AI name them, a competitor, Amazon, or the manufacturer?
2. **Glance at their site.** Is it a parts dump you can't search? Quote-only with no real product data? Note it in plain words.
3. Write one line: what the AI said, and who it named instead of them.

That line feeds your opener.

## The openers

Both openers do the same three things in order: say who I am, give a reason it's worth 20 seconds, then name the real thing I found — and hand them an easy out that isn't an admission of failure. Never open with "got a quick minute?" They've heard it from every agency that ever called, and it asks permission to say no.

### Revenue Engine opener

> "Hey, is this [name]? This is Artur — I run a small thing for [trade] shops here in [area]. Real quick and then I'll let you go: before I called I tried your main line, and it went to voicemail. I figured you were out on a job. That's actually the reason I'm calling."

Pause. Let them respond. Then:

> "Most [roofers / shops] I talk to lose more work to missed calls than to anything else, and they never see it. I put together a quick rundown of what's slipping at your place — calls, text-back time, your Google profile. Takes about 15 minutes, it's free, and the numbers are yours to keep whether you ever hire me or not. I've got Thursday morning or Friday afternoon — which is easier?"

Notes:
- No "I called twice." One attempt, stated plainly. Calling repeatedly and then bragging about it reads as a stalker or a gotcha.
- "I figured you were out on a job" hands them the non-embarrassing reason before they have to reach for one.
- You assume the next step and offer a *when*, not a *whether*.

### Industrial opener

> "Is this [name]? This is Artur — I work with distributors on how they show up when buyers search. Quick one, then I'll get out of your hair. Before I called I asked ChatGPT where to buy [their part / their category], and it sent me to Amazon and a couple shops I'd never heard of. Didn't mention you at all. You've been at this [X] years and the thing doesn't know you exist."

Pause. Let them respond. Then:

> "That's a bigger deal than it sounds, because more of your buyers are starting there before they ever call. I pulled together what I found — where you're showing up, where you're not, where the quotes are leaking. Twenty minutes, no pitch, you keep what I find. Does early next week work, or are mornings better for you?"

Notes:
- The AI is the absurd one, not them. "The thing doesn't know you exist" puts you on their side against the machine, not above them.
- "No pitch, you keep what I find" pre-empts the agency-spam reflex.

## The four comebacks every opener must survive

The openers are not self-sufficient. Each one runs straight into the same handful of replies. Full word-for-word responses live in the **Objections module** — do not improvise them. The job here is to know which comebacks are coming so the opener doesn't surprise you:

**Revenue Engine:**
- *"Who is this / how'd you get my number?"* — short, honest, who-I-am answer, then back to the leak.
- *"My wife handles the calls / we have an answering service."* — the hook isn't "you have no coverage," it's "how fast does that coverage actually call back, and what does it do at 7pm." Pivot to response time, not pickup.
- *"What's it cost?"* — see the price branch below.
- *"Just send me an email."* — see the email branch below.

**Industrial:**
- *"ChatGPT isn't how my customers buy."* — the most common reply. Don't argue it head-on. Grant it: not all of them, sure. But the share doing it climbs every quarter, and it's the part you can't see in your sales numbers — the buyer who asked the AI, didn't see you, and never called. Then back to booking.
- *"We're booked solid, don't need more work."* — good, then this isn't about more leads. It's the quotes you're already losing before they reach you, and the ones sitting un-chased. Back to booking.
- *"What's it cost?"* — see the price branch below.
- *"Just send me an email."* — see the email branch below.

## The price branch (both motions)

"I don't quote cold" said flat to a skeptic sounds like you're hiding the number. Don't refuse a ballpark — refusing one loses more deals than a wide range does. Give the range, then point at why the audit decides where they land.

**Revenue Engine:**

> "Fair question. It's a monthly thing, not a one-time project. Shops your size usually land between [$X and $Y] a month, and the audit is what tells us where in that range you fall and whether it's even worth it for you. No setup games, no marking up your ads, no long contract. That's exactly why the 15 minutes matters — I'd rather show you the numbers than guess at a price."

**Industrial:**

> "Depends on what's broken, and that's what the call is for. It runs as a monthly engagement, and most shops your size land between [$X and $Y]. The diagnostic tells us where you actually are. I'm not going to throw a number at you cold — that's how every agency that's wasted your time started."

The Revenue Engine price is deliberately not published cold (depends on trade/location/scope) and no fixed figure lives in the repo — get the current band from Artur. Same for the industrial range. Do not invent ranges.

## The email branch (both motions)

"Just send me an email" is a soft no most of the time. Don't fight it, and don't fully fold either.

> "Happy to. So I send you something you'll actually open instead of a brochure — give me 15 minutes first and I'll send the rundown built on your own numbers. If it's not worth your time after that, I'll go away and you keep what I found. Fair?"

If they still want only an email:

> "No problem. What's the best address? I'll send the short version, and I'll put your name and your trade in the subject so you know it's me and not spam."

Send it, log the callback, and let the **cold-email cadence** carry it. Note the leak you named in Apollo so the follow-up email opens on the same observation — one personalized line per step, CTA is a question, not a link. The phone and the email tell one story, not two. Never paste a raw link into the first email; deliverability on this channel is reputation-gated, and a link in a cold first touch is a spam flag.

## The gatekeeper (industrial mostly)

On the industrial side the front office answers most of the time. You don't beat a gatekeeper with a trick; you give them a reason and a name. Full handling is in the **Gatekeeper module** — do not freelance it. The one move that works: be specific, be calm, don't sound like a sales call.

> "Morning — is [owner / president's name] in? ... It's Artur, he doesn't know me yet. I'll keep it short: I found something about how the company's showing up when buyers search online, wanted to run it by him directly. If he's tied up, when's a better time to catch him?"

If the gatekeeper screens hard:

> *"What's this about?"* → "Fair to ask. I checked how the company comes up when somebody searches your products online, and there's a gap I think he'd want to see. Two minutes of his time, no pitch. Is he the right person, or does someone else handle how you show up online?"
> *"Send literature / put it in an email."* → "Happy to. Who do I send it to so it actually reaches him and not a general inbox — and when's he usually around if he has a question?"

Get the name, the better time, and whether there's a second decision-maker. Log it and move on. Don't burn the contact pushing past someone whose job is to say no — you'll need them next call.

## The daily rhythm

### Dial blocks

- **Two to three blocks a day, 60–90 minutes each.** Dial straight through. No email, no Slack, no admin inside a block.
- **One motion per block.** A Revenue Engine block or an industrial block, never mixed. Your head stays in one buyer's language.
- **Dial the local-service motion late morning and early afternoon** — that's when the owner is most likely to be the one who answers, or doesn't, which is the leak. **Industrial owners** skew earlier and later, around the front office.
- Prep is done *before* the block. You walk in with a stack of contacts that already have a leak line written.

### Building the list

- **Build the day's list the night before** — prepped, tagged by motion, leak line written. Walking into a block with raw numbers wastes the block.
- **40 dialable contacts per block** is a working assumption, not a measured figure. Every dial-math example below uses it, so keep blocks sized to it for now, then adjust once you know your real connect rate.
- Two separate Apollo lists, one per motion. Never one combined list.

### After every block: 10-minute cleanup

Right after the last call, while it's fresh:

- Log every outcome: connect / no-answer / gatekeeper / voicemail / booked / not-a-fit / callback.
- Update the contact: bad number, wrong person, "ask for [name]," "call back Tuesday after 3."
- Move booked contacts to the booked stage. Move dead numbers and disqualified contacts out of the active list so you never re-dial junk.
- Tag the under-$5M industrial leads out.

Clean the list now or you re-dial junk tomorrow.

## Dial math — planning ranges, not promises

These are **planning estimates to calibrate against your real numbers.** Founder-led, no SDR team, no power dialer. Track your own dials, connects, conversations, and books for two weeks, then replace every number below with yours. The model tells you roughly how hard to dial to hit a booking goal. It does not promise an outcome.

The table below is placeholder scaffolding to size your effort — not a benchmark. The metrics module ([08-metrics.md](./08-metrics.md)) is the source of truth on measurement, and it deliberately treats no rate as real until you have ~200 dials of your own data. If the two ever seem to disagree, trust your own log.

**Rough funnel per 100 dials (calibrate this):**

| Stage | Planning range | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Dials | 100 | Numbers actually dialed |
| Connects (a live human) | ~15–25 | Someone picked up |
| Decision-maker conversations | ~6–12 | You reached the owner / president and actually talked |
| Booked next-steps | ~1–3 | Audit or Growth Call on the calendar |

Planning rule of thumb: **roughly 40–100 dials per booked next-step**, founder-led and prepped. Treat the wide end as the honest default until your real numbers come in.

One thing the leak opener does *not* do: it doesn't change your connect rate. Whether someone picks up is about timing and the number, not your first sentence. What the opener moves is the **conversation-to-book** rate. Once you've got a live owner, naming their real leak is what turns the talk into a booked audit. So a working opener pulls you from the conservative end (~100 dials/book) toward the better end (~50). It lifts the bottom row, not the connect row.

**Run it the other way — dials needed to book N audits a week, at 40 dials/block:**

- Target: **3 booked audits a week.**
- Conservative (~100 dials/book): **~300 dials/week** → **~8 blocks/week**, call it **2 blocks/day** across 4–5 days.
- Better, once the opener's working (~50 dials/book): **~150 dials/week** → **~4 blocks/week**, comfortably **1 block/day**.
- Want **5 booked/week**, conservative? **~500 dials/week** → **~13 blocks/week**, which is **3 blocks/day, five days**. That's a full founder-dial week with no slack. If you can't hit it, grinding more volume isn't the move. Tighten list quality and lean on the leak opener so your dials-per-book drops, or accept that the audit target scales with the hours you can actually dial.

Two calibration rules:

1. **Re-baseline every two weeks** with your real connect and book rates. The first model is a placeholder.
2. **Judge the day on booked next-steps, not dials.** Dials are the input you control; books are the output that matters. If dials are high and books are zero, the problem is the opener or the list, not the dial count. Fix prep before you grind more volume.

## How to run the cockpit (one screen)

When you sit down to dial:

1. **Pick the track.** Revenue Engine block or industrial block. One motion. Pull that prepped list.

2. **Work the list top to bottom.** Each contact already has its leak line or AI-answer line written. Open with the real thing you found, get to the owner, aim at one thing: *book the next step.*
   - Revenue Engine → "About 15 minutes, free, I'll show you your own numbers and you keep them either way." That's the **Revenue Leak Audit.**
   - Industrial → "Twenty minutes, I'll walk you through what I found, no pitch." That's the **Growth Call.**

3. **Pull the right branch the second you're hit.** "I work with someone," "send me an email," "what's it cost," "we tried marketing," gatekeeper screen — don't freelance. Use the price branch and email branch above; everything else is in the Objections and Gatekeeper modules. Read the line, get back to booking.

4. **Log the outcome immediately.** Booked, callback, voicemail, not-a-fit, dead number. Update the contact while it's fresh.

5. **After the block: cleanup.** 10 minutes. Tag, move, disqualify.

**Never on a cold call:** sell the whole thing, quote a hard number as if it were final, promise a specific result, manufacture urgency, or invent proof. The proof on a cold call is the prospect's own leak you found. Book the next step. The next step is where you sell.
