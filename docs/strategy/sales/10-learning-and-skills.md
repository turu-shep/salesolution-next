# Learning the cold call — path, skills & voice

*Internal. The interactive version — where you check off the path and set your level on each skill — is at **`/sales/learn`**. This doc is the readable companion: the curriculum overview, and the full voice / English reference with sources.*

For the person this is built for: never sold on a cold call, voice still being conditioned, English as a second language. None of that is a blocker. It's a build order. Go slow, one stage at a time.

---

## A note on your voice — read this first

The warmups and habits below keep a *healthy* voice healthy. They do **not** fix an underlying problem. Since your voice already tires and strains easily, the right first move is a **one-time voice assessment with a speech-language pathologist (SLP)** — treat that as recommended, not optional.

**Stop and see an ENT / laryngologist** if you get: pain when speaking, frequent voice loss, or **hoarseness that lasts more than two weeks**. Urgent red flags (trouble breathing/swallowing, a neck lump, coughing up blood) are not a sales problem — get care. None of the drills here replace that.

## The path (8 stages)

Each stage on `/sales/learn` carries the concrete actions, voice + English notes, drills, and the "ready when" gate. The shape:

| Stage | You'll be able to… | Ready when |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Understand the game** (no dialing) | Explain what you're doing and why before you risk a call | You can say in one sentence what you're booking and why you never sell on the call; you can state the guarantee + terms verbatim |
| **1 — Get the instrument working** (voice & English) | Last a block, sound calm, be understood | A friend understands every word of your opener first try; voice fine after 30 min; a recording sounds calm |
| **2 — Put the words in your mouth** (drill the script) | Say the openers + key blocks from memory | You say the opener, price branch, and burned-buyer response from memory, cleanly |
| **3 — Simulate** (role-play, zero stakes) | Run a full mock call and handle pushback | Full mock call start to finish; 3 objections in a row without freezing |
| **4 — First live dials** (low stakes, leak-led) | Have real owner conversations; voice holds | ~50 dials, ~5 real decision-maker conversations; opener live without the voice cracking |
| **5 — Handle pushback live** (objections) | Agree, redirect, let go cleanly | Take the 6 common objections without arguing; let a soft no go after one re-ask |
| **6 — Book the next step** (the close) | Book audits / Growth Calls naturally | Booking at a steady baseline; the close feels like a small, safe yes |
| **7 — Refine & make it instinct** (ongoing) | Keep sharpening with tape and reps | Ongoing — most dashboard skills sit at "Live" or "Instinct" |

The ramp behind it: **week 1** voice + script foundations, no live dials; **week 2** delivery + role-play; **week 3** first low-stakes dials in short 20–25 min blocks, twice a day; **week 4+** grow the block toward 60–90 min only once the voice holds without tightness.

## Voice, delivery & English — the reference

*Healthy-voice and coaching guidance, not medical treatment. Sources at the bottom.*

### Vocal warmups, health & stamina

**Warm up before every block (about 5 minutes, all gentle, no pushing):**
- **Straw phonation / SOVT** (the core safe warmup — the back-pressure lets the cords vibrate with less strain): blow air through a straw (voice off) ~5×, then hum a steady "ooo" into it, then add gentle pitch glides (sirens) up and down, then "sing" a simple line into it. Don't grip the straw with teeth/lips; keep neck and shoulders relaxed.
- **No straw?** Same progression with humming, lip trills ("brrrr"), or tongue trills. A relaxed, easy buzz on the lips is the target.
- **Cool down** with 1–2 minutes of the same hum/straw after a long block, not only before.

**Daily vocal hygiene:**
- **Hydration:** ~64 oz of water spread across the day; sip steadily rather than chugging before calls. A humidifier or steam gets moisture to the folds most directly in dry/heated rooms.
- **Throat-clearing harms the cords** — sip water and swallow instead, or a silent "h" puff. Avoid drying menthol/eucalyptus lozenges.
- **Vocal rest is part of recovery** — protect quiet time around the calling day. Don't whisper to rest; soft, easy normal speech is gentler. Never push the voice through illness.
- **Posture:** sit or stand tall, shoulders down, head level — open the airway so the voice rides on breath, not the throat. Standing for calls often helps.

**Breath support:** breathe low so the **belly** expands on the inhale (not shoulders). If the breath doesn't supply the air, the larynx overworks — that's where strain comes from. Quick drill: hand on belly, inhale so it pushes out, exhale slow on "sss," 5× before each block. On calls, speak on the exhale, top up air at pauses, keep volume conversational.

**Surviving a 60–90 min block:** warm up first; pace, don't push volume; micro-rest between dials; **water + ~30–60s of silence every 20–30 minutes**; and a hard **stop signal** — if the voice feels tight or scratchy or you keep wanting to clear your throat, end the block. Pushing a fatigued voice is how damage starts.

### Pace, pausing & breath on calls

- **Slow down on purpose.** Nerves and a second language both speed you up; a deliberate, unhurried pace is the single simplest fix for clarity *and* uptalk.
- **Pause at phrase boundaries and breathe there** — a real beat after their name, after your one-line reason for calling, and before the ask. Pauses read as confidence, not hesitation.
- **Kill upspeak.** Don't end statements with a rising, question-like pitch. Drill: read your script and **drop the pitch on the last word of every statement** (↓). Save the rise for genuine questions.
- A slower, well-paused, fully-breathed delivery also **protects the voice** — you're never squeezing out the end of a sentence on empty lungs.

### Non-native English: clarity & confidence

- **Prosody beats perfect sounds.** Stress, rhythm, and intonation hurt or help intelligibility *more* than individual mispronounced sounds — so drill these first.
  - **Word stress:** stress the right syllable (com-PA-ny, a-VAIL-able, de-CI-sion).
  - **Sentence stress & rhythm:** English is stress-timed — **hit the content words** (nouns, verbs, numbers) and let the small words (the, of, to, a) go light and quick. Mark the stressed word in each line.
  - **Intonation:** falling on statements, rising on real questions.
- **Script the exact lines.** Reading near-verbatim **lowers cognitive load** — clearer speech and less strain, because you're not composing English and managing nerves at once. Build the script from words you already say cleanly; rewrite around sounds that trip you ("use," not "utilize"); short sentences, one idea per breath.
- **Accent-as-asset:** prospects don't buy "native-sounding," they respond to **clear, calm, and prepared.** A clear, well-paced accent beats fast native-speed mumbling. Speed, stress, and clarity are the work — not the accent.
- **"Sorry, say that again?"** is normal, not failure. Don't apologize or speed up — slow down and re-say it. Ready line: *"Of course — let me say that more clearly."* If one word keeps failing, swap an easier synonym.

### Beginner progression (zero → first calls)

- **Shadowing:** play a clear, slow speaker (or a good call recording) and repeat aloud with almost no delay, copying rhythm and intonation — 10–15 min/day. Builds prosody faster than silent reading.
- **Read aloud → record → self-review:** record your script, compare to a model for pace, stress, and endings. You can't fix what you can't hear.
- **Role-play** the full script, objections included, before any live dial.
- **Build stamina gradually** — don't open with a 90-minute block.
