# What an AI SEO Agency Actually Does (and How to Spot a Rebrand)

Somewhere around 2024, half the SEO agencies in the world renamed themselves. Same team, same deliverables, new word on the homepage: AI.

Some of them changed the work too. Most didn't. If you're shopping for an AI SEO agency right now, your real job is telling those two groups apart — because the invoice looks identical and the outcome doesn't.

## The work changed because the buyer changed

Your customers stopped reading ten blue links. They ask ChatGPT which vendor to use, or they read Google's AI answer and never scroll past it. The click you used to compete for often doesn't happen at all.

That breaks the old contract. A traditional SEO agency sells you position: get the page to rank #3 instead of #9. An AI SEO agency sells you something different — being the company the answer names. Those are related jobs, but they are not the same job, and the second one has deliverables the first never needed.

## What the real work includes

When the label change is real, the monthly work looks like this:

**A visibility audit that starts with prompts, not keywords.** Before anything else: ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI the questions your buyers actually ask, and record who gets named. That's your baseline. If an agency's audit is a keyword-ranking report with a new cover page, you're looking at a rebrand.

**Content built to be quoted.** AI answers lift specific, checkable statements — definitions, numbers, comparisons, clear yes/no calls. Pages written as 2,000-word mood pieces don't get lifted. Pages that state a claim plainly and back it up do. The craft is making your pages the easiest source to quote.

**A site machines can actually read.** Pricing hidden behind "request a quote," product data scattered across PDFs, pages that only render after JavaScript loads — all of it makes you invisible to the systems assembling answers. Fixing this is unglamorous and it moves the needle.

**Your footprint on other people's sites.** AI systems corroborate. A claim that exists only on your own domain is weaker than one echoed by third parties, because retrieval-based answers check sources against each other before they commit — the mechanism behind this is called grounding, and it's worth understanding before you buy. Practical translation: mentions and citations on sites the AI already trusts are part of the deliverable now, not a nice-to-have.

**Measurement in citations, not positions.** The monthly report should answer one question: for the prompts that matter, do the AI answers name you more often than they did last month? Rankings can ride along. They're no longer the headline.

## Five questions that separate the real ones

1. **"Show me a prompt-level baseline for a current client."** Real firms track named-in-answer rates. Rebrands show you Semrush.
2. **"Which of my pages would an AI quote today, and why?"** You want a specific, unflattering answer.
3. **"What do you do off my site?"** If the answer is "we focus on your content," they're missing the corroboration half of the job.
4. **"When should I expect the first movement?"** Honest answer: months, not weeks. In my own client work, a from-scratch industrial site picked up its first AI citation at week 12. Anyone promising faster is guessing or lying.
5. **"What won't you do?"** The good ones name their limits without flinching. Nobody controls what a model says. The work raises the odds; it can't script the answer.

## The one red flag that overrides everything

"Guaranteed placement in AI answers." Nobody can guarantee that. The models change, the retrieval changes, and no agency has a back door. A firm that guarantees the un-guaranteeable on page one of the pitch will be flexible with the truth on page two.

Pick the agency that talks about your buyers' questions, shows you who's getting named today, and gives you a plan measured in citations. The rebrand will be cheaper. It will also be the old product in a new box.
