# GEO Agency or SEO Agency? How to Pick When Buyers Ask AI First

Run one test before you read anything else. Open ChatGPT and ask it the question your best customer would ask before finding you — "best industrial hose supplier in the Midwest," "who should redo my roof in Tampa," whatever fits your business. Read the answer.

If it names you, close this tab. If it names your competitors, you now understand the GEO agency pitch better than most people selling it.

## Two agencies, two finish lines

An SEO agency's finish line is position: your page above the other pages when someone searches. A GEO agency's finish line is the answer itself: when an AI assembles a response, your business is one of the names in it. GEO stands for generative engine optimization, and the "generative engines" are the tools your buyers already use — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews.

The distinction matters because the searcher's behavior split. Some buyers still scroll results. A growing share reads the AI's answer and acts on it without clicking anything. Rank #1 for a query where the AI answers first, and you've won a spot below the fold of a conversation that's already over.

## Where the jobs overlap (more than either side admits)

Here's what the turf war hides: good SEO is the floor GEO stands on. AI systems find sources by crawling and retrieving — the same plumbing search engines use. A site that's slow, thin, or unreadable fails both tests at once.

The work diverges above that floor. SEO optimizes for the click: titles that get chosen, pages that hold attention. GEO optimizes for the quote: statements specific enough for a model to lift, corroboration on third-party sites so the model trusts the claim, structured data so the machine parses what you sell. An SEO report counts positions and traffic. A GEO report counts how often the answers name you.

## Which one you need

**Weight toward SEO** if your buyers are still clicking — local emergency services, purchases people want to see with their own eyes, categories where AI answers are still rare. Check by searching your own money queries and noting whether an AI answer even appears.

**Weight toward GEO** if your buyers research before buying and the AI already answers their questions — B2B and industrial purchases, professional services, anything where "who's the best…" gets asked. In these categories the answer box is the new page one.

**Most businesses need a blend**, which raises the practical question: one shop that does both, or two specialists? My bias, openly: this work punishes hand-offs. The content, the technical fixes, and the third-party footprint have to move together, and a [GEO agency](https://salesolution.net/services/ai-seo/) that owns the whole flow will usually beat an SEO vendor and an AI vendor emailing each other PDFs.

## Questions that expose a weak GEO pitch

- "Show me the before/after for a real client — named-in-answer counts, not traffic." If they can't, they haven't done the work.
- "Which AI systems will you track, and how often?" You want a named list and a monthly cadence, not "we monitor AI visibility."
- "What happens on sites that aren't mine?" No answer about third-party mentions means they're selling content marketing with a new label.
- "What's out of your control?" The honest answer is: the model's final output. Anyone who claims otherwise is selling certainty they don't have.

## The part nobody puts in the pitch deck

This market is young. Pricing is unstandardized, results compound slowly — expect the first named answers in months two to four, not week two — and some of the loudest firms in it were doing something else eighteen months ago. That cuts both ways: there are sharp small operators doing excellent work, and there are rebrands charging GEO prices for SEO deliverables.

The test from the first paragraph is your compass. Whoever you hire, ask the same buyer-questions every month and count who the AI names. If that number isn't moving by month four, the label on the agency never mattered.
