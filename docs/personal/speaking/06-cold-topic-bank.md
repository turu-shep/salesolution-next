# Cold-topic bank — zero-prep drills

**Protocol (solo):** get a random number 1–48 FIRST (phone RNG, two dice, anything). Then open this file and read **only that line**. Never browse. Browsing turns a cold drill into a rehearsed one. With Claude: just type `cold` — served topics never come from a list you've seen.

**The drill:** 60–90 seconds. Five seconds to pick the landing sentence. Speak. Forward-only, short sentences, stop on the landing. That's the whole thing — the topic doesn't matter, the assembly does.

Hard-mode topics (marked ★) are deliberately multi-clause thoughts — the 8-word sentence cap applies.

## Explain (business mechanics, plain words)
1. Explain to a 12-year-old why a business can be busy and broke at the same time.
2. Why does a $9,000 roof job start with a 40-second phone call? Walk the chain.
3. Explain what a distributor actually does — why doesn't the factory just sell direct?
4. Why do referrals dry up even when the work is excellent?
5. Explain compound interest without numbers. Use a story.
6. What's a brand, really? Explain it to a plumber who thinks it's a logo.
7. Why is "we'll call you back" the most expensive sentence in a small business?
8. ★ Explain why measuring the wrong number is worse than measuring nothing, and give two examples from different industries.
9. Explain why nobody reads websites anymore — and what they do instead.
10. Why does a job posting attract the wrong people? Explain the filter problem.
11. Explain cash flow vs profit with one week in the life of one roofer.
12. ★ Explain why the second employee is harder than the first, the tenth is harder than the second, and the fiftieth is easy.

## Opinion (take a stance, defend it, land it)
13. Most business advice is written by people who've never run one. Agree or push back.
14. Should a founder do sales forever, or fire himself from it as fast as possible?
15. Cold calling in 2026: dead, undead, or misunderstood?
16. "The customer is always right" — keep it, kill it, or fix it?
17. Is working 6 days a week discipline or bad design?
18. ★ Speed matters more than quality in the first year of anything. Defend or demolish, with the boundary where it flips.
19. AI answers will kill more small businesses than Amazon did. Where do you land?
20. Cheap clients cost more than expensive ones. True, false, or lazy thinking?
21. Should every teenager work a phone-answering job once? Why?
22. Meetings: necessary evil, pure waste, or badly run theater?
23. ★ Argue that boring industries are the best businesses in the world — then steelman the opposite for two sentences and answer it.
24. Multilingual founders have an unfair advantage nobody talks about. What is it?

## Story (a scene, told forward, with a point)
25. A time you were completely wrong in public. Scene, then what it installed in you.
26. The first money you ever earned. Where were you standing?
27. A conversation you replayed for days because the right answer came too late.
28. The moment you understood what your father's (or mother's) work actually was.
29. A machine, tool, or piece of software you genuinely loved. Make me feel it.
30. The worst phone call you ever had to make.
31. A time silence worked better than anything you could have said.
32. ★ A belief you held for ten years and dropped in one afternoon — the afternoon, then the belief, in that order.
33. The best boss or client you've had, in one scene that shows why.
34. A time you quit something and it was the right call.

## Abstract (a concept, made concrete)
35. Why is "almost done" the most dangerous phrase in any project?
36. Explain momentum — not in physics, in a life.
37. What does a good question actually do? Give one and dissect it.
38. ★ Explain the difference between simple and easy, then between complex and hard, and why people pay for the wrong one.
39. Why do people trust confidence more than competence — and when does it flip?
40. Explain what a habit is to someone who's never kept one.
41. Patience and procrastination look identical from outside. What's the tell?
42. Why does naming a thing give you power over it?

## Sell it (impromptu pitch — 60 seconds, one ask at the end)
43. Sell me a $40 umbrella when I can see it isn't raining.
44. Sell a landline phone to a 20-year-old. Seriously.
45. Sell me on skipping college — to my mother.
46. Sell a paper map to someone holding a phone.
47. ★ Sell me my own product back to me: I'm a roofer who already bought an answering service last year and hates it. (Claim gates apply even here.)
48. Sell winter. The season. To someone who hates being cold.

**Regenerate:** when these stop feeling cold (you'll know — the landing arrives before the topic finishes), tell Claude "refresh the topic bank" and this file gets 48 new ones. Don't reuse a ★ topic within two weeks.
