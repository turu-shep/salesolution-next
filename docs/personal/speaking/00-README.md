## How this pack relates to the sales playbook

The sales playbook ([docs/strategy/sales/](../../strategy/sales/)) is the *what to say* — canonical, word-for-word where it matters. This pack is the *how to train saying it*. Two systems, one rule for reconciling them:

- **Roulette and cold drills:** respond cold, landing-first, forward-only — then read the canon card aloud twice and re-record. The cold rep trains assembly; the canon rep trains the words.
- **The three verbatim blocks** (guarantee, terms, audit offer) are never improvised, in practice or live. Drilled as-written or not at all — a prospect who hears two versions of the guarantee decides you're making it up.
- The cold-call learning path at `/sales/learn` ([10-learning-and-skills.md](../../strategy/sales/10-learning-and-skills.md)) is the call-specific track; this program is the general instrument. The daily protocol's sales slots double as that path's drill time. Its voice-health guidance (straw phonation, the 2-week hoarseness rule, the SLP assessment recommendation) applies to every session here — 30 takes/day is a vocal load.

## Guardrails — what never gets drilled

Whatever gets 30 reps becomes automatic. So the practice version must already be the compliant version. Non-negotiables, from the claims library and card rules:

1. **No client names out loud.** "Northern Hydraulics" and every logo-strip name stay out of scripts and stories told to prospects (naming block: [fact-ledger.md](../../strategy/case-studies/fact-ledger.md)). The trained story says "a hydraulics distributor."
2. **No client results on a cold call, period.** The proof on a cold call is the leak you found on *their* phone or *their* AI answer. Case-study numbers belong in warm contexts only (growth calls, networking, podcasts).
3. **Numbers follow [_claims-library.md](../../strategy/sales/_claims-library.md).** "47 hours" is the industry average, cited "LeadSync, 2026," never the prospect's number. "As many as 1 in 3" keeps its hedge — the hedge is the claim. Estimate close rates stay qualitative. The $378M / 91% / 2.5x stats are industrial case-study figures — never on a Revenue Engine call.
4. **Never quote a price cold.** Both motions. Price routes to the Revenue Leak Audit or the Growth Call, and saying so is the trust signal.
5. **"Leak" is the audit's name — never a *guessed* diagnosis of the owner.** Cold, name the concrete thing: the missed call, the voicemail, the job that went to the next guy. A researched, observed leak is canon even cold — industrial 1B's "That's a leak, and it's fixable" rides on an actual AI check; it's the guess that's banned. Once *he's* said the problem out loud, canon speaks the word freely — "sounds like the leak's real" (roofing Stage 3 bridge), "where you're leaking quotes" (industrial 5A). The vendor-fragmentation mechanism line "the leak's in the seams" is canon too (operating concept; the site's shipped wording is "It's in the gaps"). The rule: never hand him "leak" as a guess, or as if it's his word before he's felt it.
6. **No jargon cold.** Never: GEO, schema, citation share, CTR, ERP, PIM, faceted navigation. Say: ChatGPT, the AI answer, "a site the AI can read," "getting found for your parts."
7. **No observed-fact bluffs.** "It named a competitor, not you" requires having run the query. "I called your line last night" requires having called. No research, no leak line.
8. **No fake urgency, ever.** No countdowns, no "spots filling." This buyer smells it and writes you off.
9. **Pronouns by motion and register.** "I" on the Revenue Engine, always — it's Artur on the phone. Industrial: "we" in writing and warm storytelling; the cold-call script speaks as "I / it's me on your account" — those blocks carry the `[we→I]` tag. Never mix pronouns inside one take.
