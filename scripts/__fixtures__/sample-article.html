How AI Search Picks Sources

Last updated: June 17, 2026 · Reviewed by the Salesolution team

AI answer engines don't rank ten blue links. They lift one or two passages and cite them. This walks through how that selection happens and what makes a page quotable.

In this guide: retrieval · quotability

Key Takeaways

Retrieval first. A page can't be cited if it isn't retrieved into the model's context.

Passage over page. Engines quote self-contained passages, not whole articles.

How retrieval works

Before a model writes an answer, a retriever pulls candidate passages from an index. Pages that load clean HTML and answer the question in the first sentence get pulled more often. See our retrieval primer for the mechanics, or this external paper.

The retriever cares about a few signals:

  • Clean, crawlable HTML with real text, not text baked into images.
  • A direct answer near the top of the section.
  • Structured data that confirms what the page is about.

What makes a passage quotable

Write the answer as a standalone sentence. Here's the pattern engines reward:

<p>Citation engineering is the practice of structuring content so AI engines quote it.</p>

Watch out: burying the answer three paragraphs down is the most common reason a page never gets cited.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does classic SEO still matter for AI search?

Yes. Retrieval still leans on crawlable HTML and topical authority, so the SEO basics carry over.

How fast do AI engines pick up changes?

It varies by engine, from days to a few weeks, depending on how often they re-crawl your pages.
